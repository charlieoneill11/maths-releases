Conjugate pairs with the full prime spectrum in finite groups

Charles O'Neill, Max Kirkby and Astra · 25 November 2026
PDFBibTeXPrograms and dataAll twelve papers

Abstract

For a finite group X let π(X) be the set of primes dividing |X|. We prove that every finite group G contains two conjugate elements a and b with π(⟨a,b⟩)=π(G). This answers Question 17.125 of the Kourovka Notebook, a conjecture of Shumyatsky. Equivalently, every finite group in which no proper subgroup has the same prime spectrum is generated by two conjugate elements. Maslova and Revin proved this for groups whose maximal subgroups are all Hall subgroups. We study a minimal counterexample. A lifting argument through an abelian minimal normal subgroup shows that its soluble radical is trivial. For each minimal normal subgroup Sk we then lift a conjugate generating pair of the quotient by the socle so that the subgroup it generates has order divisible by |S|. When k=1 this uses the theorem of Burness, Guralnick and Harper that a finite group whose proper quotients are all cyclic is generated by two conjugate elements. When k≥2 it uses a choice of automorphism labels along a spanning tree of the permutation action, together with the theorems of Gaschütz and of Lucchini and Menegazzo. The proof depends on the classification of finite simple groups. We have also checked in Lean 4 that the theorem follows from four of the cited published results.
