A prime between every two consecutive 11/5-th powers

Charles O'Neill, Max Kirkby, Astra and Fable · 24 November 2026
PDFBibTeXPrograms and dataAll ten papers

Abstract

Legendre's conjecture asserts that there is a prime between any two consecutive squares. A natural weakening replaces the exponent 2 by α>2. Chamberland and Straub showed, assuming the Riemann hypothesis, that the case α=11/5 reduces to a finite verification, and the best published statement valid for every positive integer is at α=2.2253. We prove that the interval (n11/5,(n+1)11/5) contains a prime for every positive integer n. The proof uses the Riemann hypothesis, which Alpöge has proved, so the result is unconditional. An explicit-formula argument with a compactly supported test function of twelve cosine modes, together with the Brun–Titchmarsh inequality of Montgomery and Vaughan and the theorem of Carneiro, Milinovich and Soundararajan, handles every real x≥3.5×1013. This part has also been proved in Lean 4 with Mathlib, from the Riemann hypothesis and five published results taken as hypotheses. Below that we use a computation. It certifies a prime in (m2,(m+1)2) for every m≤1014, which extends the verification of Legendre's conjecture by Sorenson and Webster by about 42%, and a prime in (n11/5,(n+1)11/5) for each of the remaining 2.97×1013 bases n<3.5×1013. Its archive is held in private storage. The certificates released with the paper, with the computation of Sorenson and Webster, cover every n≤3,883,865,444,451 and every m≤70,500,002,100,000; for the other bases the theorems rest on the verification done during the original run, of which 1.6×108 checks have been replayed independently. A final section lowers the analytic cutoff to 32,746,701,927,355, using a weighted large sieve over Farey fractions with squarefree denominators and lower bounds for the number of semiprimes obtained from the complete prime-gap histogram below 4×1018. With the first 1.4×106 zeros of ζ and the table of maximal prime gaps up to 1.32×1020, whose completeness rests on an exhaustive search not published in a refereed journal, the cutoff becomes 32,654,578,787,390.

AI use and author contributions

The mathematical arguments and computer programs in this paper were developed by Astra (GPT-6.2 Astra, OpenAI). Fable (Fable 5.2, Anthropic) worked throughout as a second model: it proposed alternative approaches, reviewed arguments and raised objections. The manuscript was prepared from the project's research records with Claude (Anthropic). All computations were re-run before release, and the programs and certificates are available at https://github.com/charlieoneill11/maths-releases.

Charles O'Neill proposed the problem because the Riemann hypothesis, now a theorem, reduces the exponent 11/5 to a finite verification, as Chamberland and Straub observed. His technical contribution was to propose closing the finite range by direct computation, and to provide the cloud computing resources for the verification described in Section 5. Charles O'Neill has checked the arguments and takes full responsibility for the correctness of the results.
