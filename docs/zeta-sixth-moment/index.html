An improved lower bound for the sixth moment of the Riemann zeta function

Charles O'Neill, Max Kirkby, Astra and Fable · 24 November 2026
PDFBibTeXPrograms and dataAll eleven papers

Abstract

Let M6(T)=∫0T|ζ(½+it)|6 dt. The conjecture of Conrey and Ghosh predicts M6(T)∼ 42 c3 T(log T)9, where c3=a3/9! and a3 is the usual arithmetic factor. We prove unconditionally that lim infT→∞M6(T) / (c3T(log T)9) ≥ 41.6406029094…, improving the previous record 41.008 of Fraccaro. The argument places every Cauchy–Schwarz test in a common Hilbert space of Schur profiles on the simplex {0<x<y<z<1}, where the target ζ3 has mass exactly 42. A lower bound then becomes the measure of the region on which physical pairings of test Dirichlet polynomials can be shown to agree with the unitary model. We first determine exactly what the two classical test families can give, namely 264076794201/6400000000=41.2619990939…. We then enlarge the admissible region using the Kuznetsov formula for marked short divisors with the level-averaged spectral large sieves of Deshouillers–Iwaniec and Watt, Heath-Brown's estimate for d3 in arithmetic progressions, and, for moduli up to T3/5, a dual-sum estimate for hyper-Kloosterman sums based on the sums-of-products theorem of Fouvry, Kowalski and Michel, combined with the bounds of Deshouillers and Iwaniec for sums of Kloosterman fractions and with the Kuznetsov formula at the level fixed by the denominators. We also show that enlarging the supports within the classes used here (the dominant shapes, the extreme traces, the shapes with a short coordinate, and the balanced and mixed-row regions, with their reflections) cannot take the projection beyond 41.9462895683…<42. The exact masses, this ceiling, the covering argument for the mixed row and the deduction of the bound from the arithmetic pairings have been checked in Lean 4 with Mathlib.

AI use and author contributions

The mathematical arguments and computer programs in this paper were developed by Astra (GPT-6.2 Astra, OpenAI). Fable (Fable 5.2, Anthropic) worked throughout as a second model: it proposed alternative approaches, reviewed arguments and raised objections. The manuscript was prepared from the project's research records with Claude (Anthropic). All computations were re-run before release, and the programs and certificates are available at https://github.com/charlieoneill11/maths-releases.

Open-weight models, Kimi K3.1 Pro (Moonshot AI) and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (DeepSeek), were able to derive a significant proportion of these results when given guidance. We will report the details separately.

Charles O'Neill proposed the problem because the Riemann hypothesis, now a theorem, gives Harper's bound M6(T)≪ T(log T)9, which controls the error terms of the Cauchy–Schwarz method at the correct order. His technical contribution was to ask for the test-function method to be set up as an optimisation problem and, if it fell short of 42, for its ceiling to be proved. This led to the exact value in Theorem 1 and to the ceiling in Proposition 3. Max Kirkby set up and ran the experiments with open-weight models. Charles O'Neill has checked the arguments and takes full responsibility for the correctness of the results.
