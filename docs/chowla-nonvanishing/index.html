Central nonvanishing of quadratic Dirichlet L-functions: beyond the one-level density bound, uniformly in local conditions

Charles O'Neill, Max Kirkby, Astra and Fable · 24 November 2026

Abstract

We show that L(½,χd)≠0 for more than 94.53419890% of fundamental discriminants d, counted by |d|≤X. This improves the bound (19-cot¼)/16=94.2730…% that follows from the one-level density with Fourier support in (-2,2). Both bounds use the generalised Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions, which Alpöge has proved, so both are unconditional. The bound holds separately in each of the six classes determined by the sign of d and its 2-adic type. It also holds uniformly after prescribing the splitting type (split, inert or ramified) at every odd prime up to z=o(log X). The method weights each zero by the square of an amplitude that is a polynomial in centred linear statistics of all the zeros. Evaluating its mean needs mixed moments of linear zero statistics of all orders with total Fourier support below two, which we compute in every class uniformly in the local conditions. The numerical certificate has also been proved in Lean 4 with Mathlib, and so has the main bound, from GRH, two classical facts about quadratic L-functions and our moment theorem without local conditions, taken as hypotheses. Finally, for d=8m>0 we determine what the first two moments of L(½,χ8m) give when they are twisted by an arbitrary function of the symbols χ8m(p) at the odd primes p≤y; this part uses neither the Riemann hypothesis nor GRH. For y=clog X, with c>0 a sufficiently small absolute constant, the optimal ratio of the squared first moment to the second moment over all such multipliers is (6eγ+o(1))loglog X/log X. Conditionally on one pattern of symbols at the odd primes up to y, where 3≤y≤clog X, the ratio is O(log(2y)/log X). For fixed y it is asymptotic to κ(y)/log X, with an explicit constant κ(y)>0. As y→∞ it is asymptotic to 6eγlog y/log X, uniformly over the patterns in which the logarithms of the prescribed ramified primes sum to at most (loglog X)3.

AI use and author contributions

The mathematical arguments and computer programs in this paper were developed by Astra (GPT-6.2 Astra, OpenAI). Fable (Fable 5.2, Anthropic) worked throughout as a second model: it proposed alternative approaches, reviewed arguments and raised objections. The manuscript was prepared from the project's research records with Claude (Anthropic). All computations were re-run before release, and the programs and certificates are available at https://github.com/charlieoneill11/maths-releases.

Open-weight models, Kimi K3.1 Pro (Moonshot AI) and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (DeepSeek), were able to derive a significant proportion of these results when given guidance. We will report the details separately.

Charles O'Neill proposed the problem because the generalised Riemann hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions, now a theorem, places every nontrivial zero of L(s,χd) on the critical line, so that the one-level density of low-lying zeros bounds the order of vanishing at the central point. His technical contribution was to direct the work to this route, asking for the exact restriction that leaves a positive proportion of possible exceptions and for an estimate that reduces it; the weighted-zero method of this paper is that estimate. Max Kirkby set up and ran the experiments with open-weight models. Charles O'Neill has checked the arguments and takes full responsibility for the correctness of the results.
