The unit conjecture fails for integral group rings

Charles O'Neill and Astra · 27 November 2026
PDFBibTeXPrograms and dataAll twelve papers

Abstract

Higman asked in 1940 whether the integral group ring of a torsion-free group has only the trivial units ±g, and Kaplansky's unit conjecture asks the same over every field. Gardam disproved the conjecture over the field with two elements, Murray over every field of positive characteristic, and Gardam over the complex numbers, with coefficients in ℤ[ζ8]. The question over the integers, and over \Q and \R, remained open. We show that the integral group ring ℤ[P] of the Promislow group P, the torsion-free group of these examples, has a non-trivial unit. The unit u is an explicit triple commutator of three units of a central localisation of ℤ[P], and its inverse is the formal inverse of that commutator, so the only thing to prove is that u and u-1 have integer coefficients. Each of the three possible denominator primes F cancels because the commutators involved are congruent to one modulo increasing powers of F after a suitable conjugation. Consequently the unit conjecture fails over every field and over ℤ. The reduction of u modulo every prime p is a non-trivial unit of 𝔽p[P], and we also give a non-trivial unit of ℤ[P] that is congruent to one modulo two. The identities used about the three letters are proved by hand and confirmed by exact computation, and the integrality of u and of its inverse has been confirmed by two independently written programs.
