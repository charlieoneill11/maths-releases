Twelve papers

Charles O'Neill, Max Kirkby, Astra and Fable · 24–25 November 2026 · Programs and data on GitHub
  • An improved lower bound for the sixth moment of the Riemann zeta function
    liminf M6(T) / (c3 T (log T)9) ≥ 41.6406, with c3 = a3/9! (previous record 41.008; conjectured 42)
  • Central nonvanishing of quadratic Dirichlet L-functions: beyond the one-level density bound, uniformly in local conditions
    L(1/2, χd) ≠ 0 for more than 94.53419890% of fundamental discriminants (previous 94.2730%); the numerical certificate also proved in Lean
  • A prime between every two consecutive 11/5-th powers
    a prime in (n11/5, (n+1)11/5) for every n ≥ 1; Legendre’s conjecture checked by computation for every m ≤ 1014 (independently replayed in part)
  • Primes below n2-δ in every residue class, for a majority of moduli
    for more than 58.7% of moduli n, every reduced class mod n has a prime below C n2−δ; the density computation also proved in Lean
  • Six colours suffice for every unit-distance graph with at most 10,689 vertices or 245,510 edges
    every unit-distance graph with at most 10,689 vertices, or at most 245,510 edges, is 6-colourable (previous 6,992 and 232,645); the edge bound also proved in Lean
  • The fourth strong power of the seven-cycle has independence number at most 112
    α(C7⊠4) ≤ 112 and α(C7⊠5) ≤ 392 (previous 115 and 401); both also proved in Lean
  • Packing side-two cubes in the three-dimensional torus of side N≡5 mod 8
    α(CN⊠3) = N(mN−1)/2 for every N = 4m+1 ≡ 5 (mod 8): the conjecture of Baumert et al. (1971) for this family, previously known when N or m is prime and within 2 in general; computer-assisted
  • Conjugate pairs with the full prime spectrum in finite groups
    every finite group G has conjugate elements a, b with π(⟨a, b⟩) = π(G), answering Shumyatsky’s Question 17.125 in the Kourovka Notebook (previously known when all maximal subgroups are Hall subgroups); the deduction from four cited theorems also proved in Lean
  • A Hadamard matrix of order 4792
    an explicit Hadamard matrix of order 4792 = 8·599, an order for which none was known before (twice 2396, the smallest open order); HHT = 4792I checked by three independent programs
  • The optimal constant for four-colour quadratic van der Waerden numbers
    W(ex2+fx; 4) ≤ 57|e|+39|f|+1 for all e, f, so the optimal constant C4 asked for by Kumthekar, Gasarch, Regli and Whitehouse is 57 (they proved 57 ≤ C4 ≤ 64); six new exact values of W(x2+jx; 4); computer-assisted, with checked DRAT proofs
  • Every denominator occurs in the upper half for the circular chromatic index
    for every q > 2 a connected regular simple graph, of degree 6 or 9, whose circular chromatic index has reduced denominator q and lies strictly between Δ+1/2 and Δ+1, answering Problem 7 of Mazák and Zrubák; every rational in [19/2, 29/3] is the circular chromatic index of a 9-regular graph; computer-assisted
  • The unit conjecture fails for integral group rings
    the integral group ring of the torsion-free Promislow group has a non-trivial unit, so the unit conjecture fails over the integers, answering Higman’s 1940 question, and over every field (non-trivial units of this group were known before only over fields of positive characteristic and over C); the unit is an explicit triple commutator, its integrality checked by two independent programs

AI use. The mathematical arguments and computer programs in six of these papers were developed by Astra (GPT-6.2 Astra, OpenAI). Fable (Fable 5.2, Anthropic) worked throughout as a second model: it proposed alternative approaches, reviewed arguments and raised objections. The manuscripts were prepared from the project's research records with Claude (Anthropic). All computations were re-run before release, and the programs and certificates are available at https://github.com/charlieoneill11/maths-releases. Charles O'Neill has checked the arguments and takes full responsibility for the correctness of the results. The papers on packing cubes, on conjugate pairs, on the Hadamard matrix of order 4792 and on quadratic van der Waerden numbers were produced differently. The cube-packing arguments and programs were developed by GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol in Codex, the conjugate-pair argument by GPT-6.2 Astra, the Hadamard construction and search by GPT-6.2 Astra, starting from Kishan Kumar’s public search programs, and the quadratic van der Waerden arguments and programs by GPT-6.2 Astra; each of their pages gives its own statement. Open-weight models, Kimi K3.1 Pro and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, were able to derive a significant proportion of the results in four of these papers when given guidance.
