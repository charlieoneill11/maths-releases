Six colours suffice for every unit-distance graph with at most 10,689 vertices or 245,510 edges

Charles O'Neill, Max Kirkby, Astra and Fable · 24 November 2026
PDFBibTeXPrograms and dataAll eleven papers

Abstract

The chromatic number of the plane is known to be 6 or 7, so a finite unit-distance graph of chromatic number 7, if one exists, must be large. We prove that every unit-distance graph in the Euclidean plane with at most 10,689 vertices is properly 6-colourable; the previous bound of this kind, due to Parts, covered graphs with at most 6,992 vertices. We also prove that every finite unit-distance graph with at most 245,510 edges is properly 6-colourable, whatever its number of vertices; the previous edge bound, 232,645, follows from numerical estimates of de Grey and Parts. Equivalently, a seven-chromatic unit-distance graph, if one exists, has at least 10,690 vertices and at least 245,511 edges. Both results come from placing a fixed periodic six-colouring of the plane at random and then repairing what little goes wrong. For the edge bound we construct an everywhere-defined measurable periodic six-colouring in which a uniformly random unit segment is monochromatic with probability less than 4.07715×10-6, and we show that a single monochromatic edge can be removed by recolouring one endpoint unless both endpoints see all six colours. A Fourier argument that is uniform over all stars shows that, at every vertex, the expected number of neighbours sharing its colour exceeds the probability that it sees all six colours by a fixed multiple of its degree. For the vertex bound we use a partial colouring with small blank holes. The cost of blank vertices is partly recovered by a forest of pairs of vertices that are blank together, chosen after the placement: nearby vertices, pairs whose difference is close to a vertical unit vector, and pairs of centres of two unit circles on each of which the same two common neighbours are consecutive. Crossing-number bounds control the degree variance of a minimal counterexample and force many such pairs of circles. Every numerical inequality is reduced to a finite computation in exact integer, rational or ball arithmetic, and the programs are published with the paper. The edge bound has also been proved in Lean 4 with Mathlib, and so has the vertex bound, from four published facts about crossing numbers taken as hypotheses.

AI use and author contributions

The mathematical arguments and computer programs in this paper were developed by Astra (GPT-6.2 Astra, OpenAI). Fable (Fable 5.2, Anthropic) worked throughout as a second model: it proposed alternative approaches, reviewed arguments and raised objections. The manuscript was prepared from the project's research records with Claude (Anthropic). All computations were re-run before release, and the programs and certificates are available at https://github.com/charlieoneill11/maths-releases.

Open-weight models, Kimi K3.1 Pro (Moonshot AI) and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (DeepSeek), were able to derive a significant proportion of these results when given guidance. We will report the details separately.

Charles O'Neill proposed the problem after the proof that the plane is not five-colourable left six colours as the open case, and framed it through random placement of a periodic partial six-colouring: if the uncoloured fraction is ε, a random translate properly six-colours any configuration of fewer than 1/ε points. That reduction underlies both theorems. Max Kirkby set up and ran the experiments with open-weight models. Charles O'Neill has checked the arguments and takes full responsibility for the correctness of the results.
