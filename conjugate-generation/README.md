# Conjugate pairs with the full prime spectrum

**Result.** Every finite group G contains two conjugate elements a and b such that the subgroup ⟨a, b⟩ has the same prime divisors as G, that is, π(⟨a, b⟩) = π(G). This answers Shumyatsky's question, Question 17.125 in the Kourovka Notebook. Equivalently, every finite group in which no proper subgroup has the same set of prime divisors is generated by two conjugate elements. Maslova and Revin proved this when all maximal subgroups are Hall subgroups (2013), and Maslova proved it for a family of groups with restricted composition factors (2015).

- Paper: [conjugate-generation.pdf](conjugate-generation.pdf). The LaTeX source is in [paper/](paper/) and builds with `pdflatex main.tex`, run twice.
- Formal proof: [lean/](lean/). Lean 4 proves that the theorem follows from four of the published results below; see "Formal proof in Lean" and the paper's section "Formal verification in Lean".
- There are no computations.

## What the proof relies on

The proof is a minimal-counterexample argument. It uses these published results:

- Burness, Guralnick and Harper, *The spread of a finite group*, Ann. of Math. 193 (2021), Corollary 4(ii): a finite group whose proper quotients are all cyclic, other than C_p × C_p, is generated by two conjugate elements;
- Lucchini and Menegazzo (1997), Theorem 1.1, on the number of generators of a group with a unique minimal normal subgroup;
- Gaschütz's lifting lemma (1955);
- the Schur–Zassenhaus theorem, Schreier's conjecture, and the cyclicity of coprime automorphism groups of simple groups (Späth and Vallejo Rodríguez, Theorem 5.1).

Schreier's conjecture, the result on coprime automorphisms and the theorem of Burness, Guralnick and Harper depend on the classification of finite simple groups.

## Formal proof in Lean

The folder [lean/](lean/) holds a Lean 4 development of about 2,000 lines in 21 modules. Its main theorem, `Shumyatsky.shumyatsky_of_inputs` in [lean/Shumyatsky/Theorem.lean](lean/Shumyatsky/Theorem.lean), proves the result for every finite group from a structure `Inputs` with four fields:

- `schreier`: Schreier's conjecture;
- `coprimeAction`: coprime automorphism groups of simple groups are cyclic;
- `conjugateGenerators`: Corollary 4(ii) of Burness, Guralnick and Harper;
- `twoGenerators`: the two-generation statement that follows from Lucchini and Menegazzo's Theorem 1.1.

Gaschütz's lemma, abelian complement conjugacy and the part of Hall's theorem that the proof uses are proved in the development. `Shumyatsky.shumyatsky_solvable` proves the soluble case with no hypotheses.

- **Pins.** Lean v4.24.0 and Mathlib v4.24.0, pinned in `lean-toolchain` and `lake-manifest.json`.
- **What you trust.** [lean/Audit.lean](lean/Audit.lean) restates the theorem with the quantifiers written out. It also checks that the main theorem, the soluble case, the block lift and the simultaneous assembly depend only on the three standard axioms (`propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`). There is no `sorry`, `axiom` or `native_decide`. So you trust the Lean kernel and the four inputs.

To replay, from `lean/`:

```sh
lake exe cache get
lake build
lake env lean Audit.lean
```

On 25 November 2026 a clean build passed and Lean's kernel replayed all 21 modules. [lean/README.md](lean/README.md) walks through the proof module by module.

The development was copied from `shumyatsky-17125/` at merge commit b182c696 of the development repository.
