# Shumyatsky’s Question 17.125

For every finite group $G$, do there exist $x,g\in G$ such that the order of $\langle x,gxg^{-1}\rangle$ is divisible by every prime dividing $|G|$? The proof gives an affirmative answer using four historical finite-group results. [Kourovka Notebook, Question 17.125](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.0300v50#page=123)

## What Lean checks

The main declaration is `Shumyatsky.shumyatsky_of_inputs`. Its argument `Inputs` consists of the four statements below. Lean proves the finite-group implication from those inputs. An unconditional kernel proof for arbitrary finite groups would also need Lean proofs of those statements. The soluble theorem `Shumyatsky.shumyatsky_solvable` has only the usual group, finiteness and solubility hypotheses. [Theorem statements and proofs](Shumyatsky/Theorem.lean) · [Expanded statement and axiom checks](Audit.lean)

| Field of `Inputs` | Historical statement used |
| --- | --- |
| `schreier` | The outer automorphism group of a nonabelian finite simple group is soluble, by Schreier’s theorem. [Späth–Vallejo Rodríguez, §5](https://www.uv.es/cavaro3/download/SVCoprime.pdf#page=21) |
| `coprimeAction` | A group of automorphisms of a nonabelian finite simple group whose order is coprime to the group order is cyclic. [Späth–Vallejo Rodríguez, Theorem 5.1](https://www.uv.es/cavaro3/download/SVCoprime.pdf#page=20) |
| `conjugateGenerators` | A nonabelian finite group with every proper quotient cyclic has a conjugate generating pair. Nonabelianness excludes the corollary’s exception $C_p\times C_p$. [Burness–Guralnick–Harper, Corollary 4(ii)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.01421#page=3) |
| `twoGenerators` | In $\operatorname{Aut}(S)$ the preimage $L$ of a subgroup of $\operatorname{Out}(S)$ generated by two specified elements is two-generated. Indeed $\operatorname{Inn}(S)$ is its unique minimal normal subgroup. A normal subgroup disjoint from it centralizes it, and an automorphism centralizing every inner automorphism fixes the centerless group $S$ pointwise. Thus Theorem 1.1 gives $d(L)=\max(2,d(L/\operatorname{Inn}(S)))=2$. The same paper’s Result 1 also gives the required coset conclusion directly. [Lucchini–Menegazzo, p. 174](https://www.numdam.org/item/RSMUP_1997__98__173_0.pdf#page=3) |

## Proof audit

Choose a smallest subgroup with the original prime spectrum. Every subgroup of it with full spectrum equals the whole group. This minimality passes to quotients by taking inverse images. Induct on group order and lift a conjugate generating pair from the quotient by a minimal normal subgroup $N$. It suffices to retain generation modulo $N$ and capture the primes of $N$. The formal proof lifts through one minimal normal subgroup at a time. Applying the same induction to the quotient accounts for all the other factors with the same final pair. [Reduction](Shumyatsky/Spectrum.lean) · [Normal-subgroup induction](Shumyatsky/Induction.lean)

If $N$ is abelian, its order is a power of a prime $p$. A proper subgroup generated by a lifted pair misses $p$ and maps bijectively to the quotient. If a nonidentity $v\in N$ centralizes $x$, the lift $xv$ has order divisible by $p$. Otherwise the original complement is the unique complement containing $x$, and a suitable $N$-conjugate of the second generator leaves it. The formalization proves the abelian Hall complement conjugacy used here by averaging the difference of two splittings. [Abelian lift](Shumyatsky/Abelian.lean) · [Complement conjugacy](Shumyatsky/Complements.lean) · [Maslova–Revin, Proposition 6](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/timm977)

For nonabelian $N$, the conjugates of a minimal normal subgroup of $N$ give its decomposition as $S^k$. Lean proves the decomposition, proves that automorphisms permute the simple factors, and constructs their source-indexed labels. Minimal normality gives transitivity. Since $N$ acts trivially on the index set, generation of the quotient gives joint transitivity of the two generators. [Decomposition](Shumyatsky/Decomposition.lean) · [Coordinates](Shumyatsky/Coordinates.lean) · [Action on a minimal normal subgroup](Shumyatsky/Chief.lean)

For $k\ge2$, on an $x$-cycle choose an anchor and reserve the incoming edge of the corresponding $y$-cycle. All other $y$-labels can be prescribed in their outer cosets. Going around a cycle of length $m$ gives $(y^m)_{g(i)}=g_i(x^m)_i g_i^{-1}$. Lean constructs the conjugator along increasing distance from the anchor. The intertwining equation forces the remaining label and recovers its original outer image. [Cycle equation](Shumyatsky/Labels.lean) · [Reconstruction](Shumyatsky/Reconstruction.lean)

Two selected edges supply independent walk labels. With two or more $x$-cycles, take one edge from each of two distinct cycles. The fixed edges still connect all vertices because the rest of each affected cycle connects the endpoints of its missing edge. With one $x$-cycle, its remaining edges already connect the graph, leaving a free $y$-edge for the second choice. This includes a cycle of length two, whose directed edges have different sources. Root paths made entirely of fixed edges multiply each selected label on the left and right by fixed automorphisms. The two multiplications give independent bijections of the prescribed outer cosets. Gaschütz’s lemma supplies the ordered generating lifts, including for a repeated outer image. Lean proves that lemma by counting generating tuples in subgroups. Each resulting closed walk represents an actual word fixing the chosen factor. Its restriction supplies the required order divisibility. [Paths](Shumyatsky/Paths.lean) · [Block lift](Shumyatsky/Block.lean) · [Gaschütz’s lemma](Shumyatsky/Gaschutz.lean) · [Factor stabilizer](Shumyatsky/FactorAction.lean)

For $k=1$, the almost-simple action image $L$ inherits spectrum minimality. Its soluble outer quotient has a subgroup capturing the primes outside $\pi(S)$ and having order coprime to $|S|$. Its preimage in $L$ has full spectrum and equals $L$. Schur–Zassenhaus and the coprime-action theorem make the outer quotient cyclic. Every nontrivial normal subgroup of $L$ contains $S$, so every proper quotient is cyclic. Apply the conjugate-generation theorem. The one-entry case of Gaschütz inside the cyclic subgroup generated by the first element realizes its prescribed outer generator. Multiplying the conjugator on the right by a power of this element realizes its prescribed image as well. [Soluble subgroup argument](Shumyatsky/Hall.lean) · [Almost-simple group](Shumyatsky/AlmostSimple.lean) · [Both prescribed cosets](Shumyatsky/Cyclic.lean)

The original simultaneous assembly also checks independently of the choice to induct through a single block. Every requested inner correction on a block comes from an element of that minimal normal subgroup. Distinct minimal normal subgroups centralize one another, including isomorphic ones. Multiplying the corrections for each generator and conjugator realizes all actions together. Full spectrum then forces generation in the minimal subgroup. [Simultaneous assembly](Shumyatsky/Assembly.lean)

The independent reviewer found no change to the conclusion. Two details mattered in the proposed spanning-tree proof. The two directed edges of a 2-cycle stay distinct when forming the multigraph. If a reserved label occurs in the middle of a cycle product, write the closure equation as $A r B=C$ and solve it as $r=A^{-1} C B^{-1}$. The Lean reconstruction chooses the anchor immediately after the reserved edge.

## Reproduce

From this directory, with the toolchain in `lean-toolchain` installed, run the following commands. The manifest pins mathlib and its transitive dependencies.

```sh
lake exe cache get
lake build
lake env lean Audit.lean
```

The axiom audit checks the general implication, the soluble specialization, the block lift and the simultaneous assembly. Each uses only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` as Lean axioms. The four literature inputs are visible in the type of the general theorem.
